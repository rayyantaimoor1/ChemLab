# UI.3 — Liquids and solids

Screenshots at 1366×768, made with `npx electron scripts/capture-ui.mjs UI.3`.
Compare with `../UI.2/` for the before.

## UI.3.2 — Clarity and depth of colour

`clarity-depth.png` sets out, from left to right:

- the same copper(II) sulfate solution in a narrow test tube, a 250 mL beaker
  and a small conical flask: pale blue in the tube, deep blue in the wider glass;
- milky latex in the small beaker, which hides the bench behind it;
- cloudy starch solution in the boiling tube, which is translucent;
- starch with iodine in the second test tube: opaque blue-black.

Every label still shows the curated colour and its name. Only the drawing
changes with the glass. The other shots are the earlier set-ups, now with
this paint.

## UI.3.3 — Solids that look like what they are

`shelf-solids.png` has seven solids straight off the shelf, each drawn by its
curated form:

- marble chips in the beaker
- blue copper(II) sulfate crystals in the small beaker
- a magnesium ribbon leaning up the boiling tube
- a heap of sulfur powder in the evaporating dish
- zinc granules in a test tube
- iron filings in the small flask
- sodium hydroxide pellets in the second test tube

Each vessel's label names the solid with its colour and form.

## UI.3.4 — Precipitates by texture, solids by kind

`textures.png` shows a reaction's solid drawn by its curated texture:

- fine barium sulfate: a white layer, with the liquid still hazy
- crystalline lead(II) iodide on the bottom
- curdy silver chloride
- gelatinous iron(III) and copper(II) hydroxides hanging in the lower liquid
- copper deposited by zinc in the small beaker
- in the 250 mL beaker, copper sulfate with the current on: the cathode is
  coated in copper-coloured metal

## UI.3.5 — Colour that spreads and fades

`mixing.png` shows potassium thiocyanate just poured into iron(III) chloride in
a beaker, before anyone has stirred. The blood red comes down from the
surface as a plume, and the rest of the yellow-brown liquid is only part-way
there. The label already gives the final colour, "blood red", because the
reaction has happened; the drawing shows the liquid still mixing. Stir
finishes it, and left alone it finishes in a few seconds.

## UI.3.6 — Two layers

`layers.png` shows:

- water above tetrachloromethane in a boiling tube and a small beaker, with
  the bright boundary line between them;
- benzene floating on bromine water, having taken its orange, with the water
  underneath now colourless.

Each label names the layers in order. Pressing Stir shakes them into a cloudy
emulsion that separates again; that is checked in the running app, not shown
here. The selected beaker's large label covers part of the tubes in this shot,
the same UI.1 label-placement limit as before.

## Note on UI.3.4

In a crowded set-up like this one, a colour or texture that matches the
liquid it is in is hard to see at bench size. The close look (UI.7) will show
it large. On a bench
this crowded, some labels still overlap the glass; that is the UI.1
label-placement limit, not new here.
